## How I Approach Technology

### Solve the workflow first

Technology should address a real operational problem.

I do not believe in adding AI simply because AI is available. The first questions should be:

**What is the problem?**

**Where is the friction?**

**What should the workflow look like?**

Then the appropriate technology can be designed around that answer.

### Fewer unnecessary steps

Healthcare professionals already work in complex environments.

Good software should reduce unnecessary clicks, repetitive work, duplicate entry, fragmented systems, and avoidable interruptions.

### Technology should adapt to the organization

Organizations should not have to redesign themselves around poorly designed software.

Whenever possible, software should adapt to the organization's workflow, policies, systems, users, and operational reality.

### Human judgment remains important

AI can assist with research, analysis, automation, coordination, and decision support.

It should not remove appropriate human oversight or professional judgment from decisions where those things matter.

## Engineering Principles

A few principles guide most of my work:

- Build around real problems, not technology trends.
- Make workflows simpler rather than more complicated.
- Treat security and privacy as architecture, not afterthoughts.
- Prefer clear system boundaries and explicit authority.
- Automate repetitive work while keeping appropriate human control.
- Design systems that can be understood, tested, maintained, and improved.
- Use AI where it provides meaningful value.
- Build for the people who actually have to use the software.
